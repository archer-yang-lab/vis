The update. Let π be a target density, q(y | x) a proposal density, and xt−1 the current state at iteration t ≥ 1. Write x = xt−1. Draw y ∼ q(· | x) and, independently, u ∼ Uniform(0, 1). Define

α(x,y)=min{1,π(y)q(x|y)π(x)q(y|x)}.

Set xt = y if u < α(x, y), and xt = x otherwise. Start where π(x₀) > 0. A proposal outside the target support has π(y) = 0 and is rejected.

Why the correction appears. The forward and reverse accepted probability flows agree:

π(x)q(y|x)α(x,y)=min{π(x)q(y|x),π(y)q(x|y)}=π(y)q(x|y)α(y,x).

Together with the probability of staying at x after rejection, this makes π stationary. A suitable proposal must also allow the chain to explore the target support; stationarity alone does not guarantee useful exploration in a finite run. If π(x) = f(x)/Z with unknown constant Z, the ratio π(y)/π(x) = f(y)/f(x) does not require Z.

Proposal choices. Here N(m, v) denotes a Gaussian distribution with mean m and variance v. The Gaussian random walk uses y = x + √v z, where z ∼ N(0, 1). The uniform random walk uses y ∼ Uniform(x − √(3v), x + √(3v)); it also has variance v. Both are symmetric, so q(x | y)/q(y | x) = 1. For the independent Gaussian proposal, y ∼ N(m₀, v) regardless of x; its correction is q(x)/q(y), which generally does not cancel.

Target choices. The Gaussian target has adjustable mean and variance. The mixture is w N(−m, τ²) + (1 − w)N(m, τ²), where w is the weight of the left component, ±m are its two means, and τ² is their common variance. The Gamma target has shape k, scale θ, and density π(x) = xk−1 exp(−x/θ) / [Γ(k)θk] for x > 0, with Γ(k) = ∫₀∞ zk−1e−z dz. Its mean is kθ.

Read all three views together. The density plot shows the proposal before its decision. The trace shows repeated states after rejection and slow movement between peaks. The histogram includes every retained state, including repeats; keeping only accepted proposals would generally change the sampled distribution. After discarding B iterations, it uses xB+1, …, xt. Bin heights are counts divided by the full retained count and bin width; any states outside the visible range are reported.

Try the variance control. A very small v often gives high acceptance but slow exploration. A very large v can give frequent rejections. On the mixture target, compare v = 0.05, 1, and 16, using the same starting point and seed. Inspect visits to both peaks as well as the acceptance rate. Warm-up removal and an attractive histogram do not by themselves establish convergence.
