A fixed target distribution. Let z ∈ {1, 2} be a component label, x ∈ ℝ an observation, and N(x | μ, v) the Gaussian density with mean μ and variance v. The fixed parameters are θ = (π₁, π₂, μ₁, μ₂, v₁, v₂), with π₁ + π₂ = 1, πₖ > 0, and vₖ > 0. The joint and marginal densities are

p(x,z=k|θ)=πkN(x|μk,vk), p(x|θ)=∑k=12πkN(x|μk,vk).

Two conditional draws. Given the current xt, first draw a label zt+1 with probabilities

P(zt+1=k|xt,θ)=πkN(xt|μk,vk)∑j=12πjN(xt|μj,vj).

Then draw xt+1 ∼ N(μzt+1, vzt+1). The probability bars show the first conditional. The dashed curve shows the second conditional, which integrates to one; it is not multiplied by the component weight. The target mixture stays fixed throughout.

Why the chain can stay in one component. Near μ₁, if the second Gaussian has very little density, P(z = 1 | x, θ) is close to one. The next x is therefore likely to come from component 1 again. Try the separated preset and then increase the variances: more overlap makes component changes easier. Long runs in one color show this dependence. These Gibbs updates preserve the joint target, but a short run can poorly represent its mixture weights.

Independent sampling is also possible here. With known parameters, drawing z from (π₁, π₂) and then x from its Gaussian gives an independent sample from the mixture. Gibbs sampling instead draws z conditional on the current x, which creates dependence across iterations. This example isolates that distinction; no model parameters are estimated.

Reading the summaries. One iteration contains both draws. After discarding B complete iterations, the histogram uses xB+1, …, xt, excluding the initial x₀. The component-1 fraction uses the associated sampled labels, not a threshold on x. Histogram heights are counts divided by the full retained count and bin width; the trace shows the last 300 states.
