Observations stay fixed; parameters change. For i = 1, …, n, let xᵢ ∈ ℝ be observed and zᵢ ∈ {1, 2} its unobserved component label. Write θ = (π, μ, v), where π = (π₁, π₂), μ = (μ₁, μ₂), and v = (v₁, v₂). Conditional on θ, observations are independent, with P(zᵢ = k | θ) = πₖ and xᵢ | zᵢ = k, θ ∼ N(μₖ, vₖ). Here N(m, V) denotes a Gaussian distribution with mean m and variance V.

Independent priors. Use π₁ ∼ Beta(α, α), π₂ = 1 − π₁, μₖ ∼ N(m₀, V₀), and vₖ ∼ IG(a₀, b₀), independently for k = 1, 2. The constants α, V₀, a₀, and b₀ are positive. The inverse-Gamma parameterization is p(v | a, b) ∝ v−a−1 exp(−b/v), v > 0. For an inverse-Wishart prior with scale S₀ and degrees of freedom ν₀, its one-dimensional version IW(S₀, ν₀) is IG(ν₀/2, S₀/2). The mean prior is independent of the variance; its variance is V₀, not V₀vₖ.

Four updates per sweep. All quantities on the right below use the most recently available values. In a conditional distribution, “rest” means the observations and all other unknown quantities. First draw each zᵢ independently conditional on θ, using the two probabilities proportional to πₖ N(xᵢ | μₖ, vₖ). For the new labels, define nₖ = ∑ᵢ 1{zᵢ = k} and sₖ = ∑ᵢ: zᵢ=k xᵢ. Then draw the weights:

π1|z∼Beta(α+n1,α+n2),π2=1−π1.

For each mean, define the conditional variance Vₖ and mean mₖ by

Vk=(1V0+nkvk)−1,mk=Vk(m0V0+skvk).

Draw μₖ | rest ∼ N(mₖ, Vₖ), independently for k = 1, 2. Finally, using these newly drawn means, draw the variances independently:

vk|rest∼IG(a0+nk2,b0+12∑i:zi=k(xi−μk)2).

If nₖ = 0, both the mean and variance conditionals reduce to their priors. Every update is a random draw. Replacing these draws with conditional means would produce a different algorithm. These Gibbs updates preserve the joint posterior p(z, θ | x); the starting values are not assumed to be posterior draws.

Fitting the density. Let θ(1), …, θ(M) denote the retained parameter draws after discarding B sweeps. For a new observation x*, the green curve approximates the posterior predictive density by

p(x*|x)≈1M∑m=1M∑k=12πk(m)N(x*|μk(m),vk(m)).

The purple curve uses only the current parameter state. No true parameters or generating labels are supplied to the sampler. Generate another dataset or paste observations, adjust the priors, and inspect how the inferred mixture changes.

Labels and uncertainty. Exchanging component numbers leaves the mixture density unchanged. The sampler retains its original labels; only the traces and posterior summaries order each draw by its two means. Reported intervals are empirical 5th–95th percentiles of these ordered draws, not intervals for a fixed arbitrary label. Draws are dependent, and discarding warm-up does not by itself ensure convergence. Try different seeds and longer runs, particularly for overlapping components.
